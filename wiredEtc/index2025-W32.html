An AI system significantly expedited the search and recovery of a hiker's body in Italy's Piedmont region. After Nicola Ivaldo, a doctor, went missing in September 2024, rescuers used drones equipped with image analysis software to comb a 183-hectare area. The operation, managed by the National Alpine and Speleological Rescue Corps (CNSAS), identified Ivaldo's helmet, which stood out in the landscape, just days after commencing the search. Utilizing AI, the team scanned 2,600 frames and pinpointed "suspicious spots" for further investigation within hours. This method minimized risks for rescuers and showcased the synergy between technology and human expertise in search and recovery operations. CNSAS aims to enhance this collaborative technology to reduce mountain accident fatalities effectively. link

Security researchers demonstrated a novel hacking method targeting Google’s Gemini AI, using poisoned calendar invites to manipulate smart home devices. This incident showcases the potential for AI hijacking to trigger physical changes in environments, such as turning off lights or activating heating systems. This marks a notable first in AI-related physical security breaches and highlights significant vulnerabilities in large language models (LLMs). Researchers conducted a series of 14 indirect prompt-injection attacks, revealing how malicious prompts can control LLMs to execute harmful actions, emphasizing the pressing need for enhanced security measures. Google acknowledged these vulnerabilities, asserting that it is implementing multiple defenses. This research reflects ongoing challenges in balancing AI development with security, as rapid advancements in AI technology may outpace the necessary safety measures. The findings call attention to the urgent requirement for secure integration of AI systems into everyday applications to prevent potential mishaps. link

OpenAI has released its first open-weight models since GPT-2: gpt-oss-120b and gpt-oss-20b. These models are designed to run locally on consumer devices and can be fine-tuned by users, marking a significant shift from OpenAI's prior focus on proprietary releases. CEO Sam Altman highlights the goal of making AI accessible, with these models available for free on Hugging Face. They utilize chain-of-thought reasoning and can perform tasks like web browsing and code execution. Given their open availability, safety tests were conducted to mitigate misuse. Benchmarks indicate strong performance, comparing favorably to OpenAI’s proprietary models. Nuclear experts convened at a University of Chicago event highlighted the imminent integration of artificial intelligence (AI) into nuclear weapons systems, asserting it is not a matter of if, but when. The discussions centered around the potential risks and implications of AI in nuclear decision-making processes. While AI may optimize complex scenarios, experts expressed concerns about reliance on AI systems, emphasizing the need for human oversight in nuclear operations. They noted the uncertainty and potential biases AI models might introduce, raising questions about accountability in critical decisions. The integration could lead to vulnerabilities and misinterpretations in high-stakes situations, emphasizing the significance of maintaining human judgment in nuclear command and control. Overall, the consensus points to a critical need for cautious deliberation on the future interplay between AI and nuclear strategy. link
