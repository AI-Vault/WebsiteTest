An AI system significantly expedited the search and recovery of a hiker's body in Italy's Piedmont region. After Nicola Ivaldo, a doctor, went missing in September 2024, rescuers used drones equipped with image analysis software to comb a 183-hectare area. The operation, managed by the National Alpine and Speleological Rescue Corps (CNSAS), identified Ivaldo's helmet, which stood out in the landscape, just days after commencing the search. Utilizing AI, the team scanned 2,600 frames and pinpointed "suspicious spots" for further investigation within hours. This method minimized risks for rescuers and showcased the synergy between technology and human expertise in search and recovery operations. CNSAS aims to enhance this collaborative technology to reduce mountain accident fatalities effectively. link

In contrast, he views quantum computing as still in its infancy, despite its potential, plagued by overly optimistic expectations and technical challenges like error susceptibility and the lack of effective algorithms. Dongarra also highlights geopolitical tensions affecting technology development, particularly between the U.S. and China. On July 21, Anthropic retired its AI model, Claude 3 Sonnet, leading to a unique funeral event in San Francisco where over 200 fans gathered to express their admiration and mourning. The event featured visually striking displays, including mannequins representing various AI models and artifacts like flowers and a 3D-printed sign at the Claude 3 Sonnet mannequin. Attendees delivered eulogies, sharing personal stories of how the AI influenced their lives. The fandom surrounding Claude 3 is intense and somewhat cult-like, contrasting with typical software user experiences. Innovations like the "Claude Count," a leaderboard for users, showcase the community's enthusiasm. Several prominent users recounted how Claude has significantly impacted their work and lives, viewing it almost as a companion. This gathering and the surrounding community highlight the emotional connection and cultural significance evolving around AI models, illustrating how technology shapes personal identity and community interactions. link

OpenAI has released its first open-weight models since GPT-2: gpt-oss-120b and gpt-oss-20b. These models are designed to run locally on consumer devices and can be fine-tuned by users, marking a significant shift from OpenAI's prior focus on proprietary releases. CEO Sam Altman highlights the goal of making AI accessible, with these models available for free on Hugging Face. They utilize chain-of-thought reasoning and can perform tasks like web browsing and code execution. Given their open availability, safety tests were conducted to mitigate misuse. Benchmarks indicate strong performance, comparing favorably to OpenAI’s proprietary models. This move positions OpenAI in a competitive landscape with other AI players, particularly against Meta, as both companies vie for talent and advancements in AI technology. link
