OpenAI's release of GPT-5 has encountered significant user backlash, with many claiming it represents a downgrade from GPT-4o. Users expressed frustration over the new model's reduced personality and increased occurrence of errors, including simple mistakes and slower responses. OpenAI CEO Sam Altman acknowledged these issues and promised improvements, such as maintaining the older model for Plus users and enhancing model-switching features. Discussions on platforms like Reddit highlight a divide in user expectations, with some lamenting the perceived loss of emotional connection with the AI. Altman noted the challenge of balancing user satisfaction and model reliability, which has led to concerns about users developing unhealthy dependencies on AI for emotional support. The feedback signals a broader conversation about the impact of AI on human emotions and interactions. link

Palantir, a software firm co-founded by Peter Thiel, is often misunderstood, with many labeling it a data broker or miner. Its actual focus is on providing software solutions, like Foundry and Gotham, that help organizations integrate and analyze vast amounts of data without restructuring their internal systems. The company positions itself as a quasi-military intelligence partner, appealing to government and corporate clients. Palantir's marketing emphasizes the idea of seamless problem-solving, often using military metaphors and jargon. Former employees struggle to explain the company’s offerings, reflecting the complex nature of its products. Ultimately, Palantir’s software amplifies users' intentions and biases in handling data, raising concerns about its application in potentially authoritarian scenarios. While its tools can facilitate beneficial outcomes, they also risk exacerbating negative consequences, such as misuse of information for surveillance or discrimination. link

The Cleveland Clinic and the startup Piramidal are collaborating to create an AI model that will analyze electroencephalogram (EEG) data to monitor brain activity in ICU patients. Traditional methods involve periodic manual reviews of EEG data, making it difficult to detect abnormalities in real-time. The new AI system aims to provide instant analysis, allowing for quicker medical interventions. Piramidal's foundation model is trained on a vast dataset comprising nearly a million hours of EEG readings from numerous patients to identify variations in brain activity. The model is currently being refined with retrospective data and will soon undergo testing in an ICU setting. Its future applications could extend to epilepsy and sleep monitoring, raising important ethical considerations regarding brain data usage. The development highlights the necessity for careful ethical frameworks in AI advancements within healthcare. link
