# 127. Hoe we talent en het grote geld binnen Europese grenzen kunnen houden (2025-10-09) [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NCPG11rsCIo)


 # Overview
The conversation is a Dutch podcast episode from BNR’s "Technoloog" where the host and guest Eline van Beest discuss the role, impact, and challenges of start-up incubators in the innovation ecosystem in the Netherlands. The discussion centers on Jes Delft, a prominent incubator in Delft that has helped launch numerous tech start-ups, plus the interplay between academia, entrepreneurship, and government policy.

# Major Themes
• Policy and continuity in support for innovation  
• The critical role of start-up incubators in bridging research and market impact  
• The importance of network effects and collaboration in early-stage ventures  
• Balancing risk-sharing between government, investors, and entrepreneurs  
• Competition with international markets (e.g., USA and China) in scaling technology

# Use Cases and Projects
• Eline’s Personal Journey:  
  – Originated from a medical device innovation for treating sleep apnea (Night Balance)  
  – Faced challenges turning a university invention into a commercial product  
  – Used Jes Delft’s resources, mentorship, and events to develop her business plan, identify co-founders (finding a partner through a simple flyer), and approach potential investors  
• Start-up Successes Mentioned:  
  – Examples like Lobster Robotics and Swapfiets illustrate how tech projects started small but went on to become household names or significant industry players  
• Intervision Groups and Collaboration:  
  – Entrepreneurs forming intervision groups eventually led to the creation of investment funds to support newer start-ups, illustrating the ripple effect of early collaboration

# Tools and Incubators
• Jes Delft Incubator:  
  – A large-scale facility at TU Delft hosting hundreds of start-ups in a hive-like setting  
  – Offers physical workspaces, specialized programs, mentorship, and networking events  
  – Helps translate university research into commercially viable innovations  
• Technology Ecosystem Support:  
  – The platform provides access to pitching events, guidance on legal and regulatory matters (e.g., designing a Quality Management System compliant with FDA requirements), and shared experiences that accelerate learning  
  – The incubator model is compared to similar successful innovation hubs abroad (e.g., Silicon Valley, Oxford) where close academia-industry collaborations are prevalent

# Collaboration Between Academia and Industry
• The discussion highlights the traditional disconnect between university research and market commercialization.  
• Universities hold the patents and intellectual property but often lack the know-how to scale innovations to production and commercialization.  
• There is a call for more dedicated courses and exposure for students in entrepreneurship, so that they become better equipped to transition research breakthroughs to market-ready products.  
• Successful examples, like partnerships with companies such as ASML and collaborations with various technical universities, are used to illustrate both existing strengths and areas for improvement.

# Policy, Risk Sharing, and International Competition
• The need for a consistent national technologiestrategy in the Netherlands is emphasized so that long-term policies are maintained despite changing political landscapes.  
• A call is made for programs such as the “3% R&D rule” and other funding initiatives to ensure that the start-up ecosystem receives steady financing.  
• The discussion touches on proposals for risk sharing, including the idea that government and institutional investors (e.g., pension funds) should help underwrite some of the risks undertaken by entrepreneurs.  
• International competition is debated, noting that US companies receive much higher funding and face fewer regulatory hurdles, thereby accelerating their growth. This situation pressures Dutch and European companies to find ways to scale effectively while preserving the benefits of their ecosystem and collaborative culture.

# Future Outlook and Recommendations
• Emphasis on maintaining stable government support and predictable policy measures to attract long-term investments in national technology sectors.  
• Encouragement of closer collaboration between industry and academia through joint projects, internships, and cross-over programs – enabling academic research to be translated into scalable products.  
• A desire for a “fast lane” mechanism for validated technologies that have already raised significant capital, so that bureaucratic delays do not hinder further growth.  
• Discussion of the need for a cultural shift towards greater risk-taking among investors and entrepreneurs, learning from the American venture capital model without losing Europe’s collaborative strengths.  
• Recognition that a balanced approach—combining ecosystem building with smart, targeted investments—could help Dutch start-ups maintain their competitive advantage in a global market.

# AI News and New Workflows
While the podcast focused primarily on start-up incubation and technology scaling challenges, it does not delve deeply into specific new AI tools or workflows. However, the discussion implicitly underlines that well-supported ecosystems (including incubators like Jes Delft and collaborative models from academia) could be readily applied to AI and other emerging technologies. The points about specialized programs in areas such as AI within Jes Delft indicate that as these technologies mature, similar approaches will be crucial for rapidly commercializing AI innovations in the Netherlands.
